## Opening

**Interviewer:**  
Thanks for doing this. I'm doing a uni project about how people come across music in everyday life. There are no right answers—I'm interested in your stories. It'll take about 15 minutes. Is it OK if I record this? It's only for our team, and your name won't appear anywhere.

**Participant:**  
Yeah, that's fine. You can record it.

## Warm-up

**Interviewer:**  
Great, thanks. To start, where do you usually listen to music during a normal week?

**Participant:**  
Um, mostly on the bus, I think, or when I'm walking to class. Sometimes when I'm coding, but only if it doesn't have lyrics. Otherwise I start listening to the words and completely forget what I'm doing. I also play music when I'm cooking, but I don't cook that often, so... yeah.

## Question 1 — A place where a song is stuck for you

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a place—a room, a shop, a walk, a bus route—where a specific song or artist is stuck for you. What happened there?

**Participant:**  
There's a Tame Impala song, *The Less I Know the Better*. I always connect it with the 66 bus for some reason. I listened to it a lot in my first semester, usually when I was going home after a late class. Nothing particularly interesting happened. I was probably just tired. The bus was always crowded and really cold because of the air conditioning, and I think I kept playing the same playlist because I couldn't be bothered choosing something else.

**Interviewer:**  
Was it the place itself, or how you were feeling at the time, that made it stick?

**Participant:**  
Um, probably both? I don't know. I was tired and a bit stressed, but I also heard it on that exact bus so many times. It just became part of the trip, I guess.

**Interviewer:**  
What happens now when you go back there?

**Participant:**  
Usually nothing. I still take that bus sometimes. But if the song comes on, I suddenly remember sitting near the back with my laptop bag on my knees. It's weirdly specific.

## Question 2 — Music chosen by a person

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a time you were somewhere and could tell the music had been chosen by an actual person rather than an algorithm. How did you know?

**Participant:**  
Probably when my flatmate was cooking. He was using the kitchen speaker, and it went from a video game soundtrack to ABBA and then suddenly to really heavy metal. So, yeah, obviously a person chose that. Or maybe "chose" is too generous. He was just playing whatever entered his head.

**Interviewer:**  
What did you end up thinking about whoever chose it?

**Participant:**  
I thought he had terrible taste. Well, not terrible exactly, just no consistency at all. I asked what the playlist was called, and he said it wasn't a playlist. He was searching for each song individually.

**Interviewer:**  
Did it matter whether you knew that person?

**Participant:**  
Yeah, probably. Because I knew him, it was funny. If I heard the same thing in a cafe, I'd probably get annoyed and leave.

## Question 3 — Something a stranger left behind

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the last time you came across something a stranger had left behind for others to find—a note, a book in a street library, a message on a wall, anything.

**Participant:**  
Um... I found a note in a library book a few weeks ago. It was written on an old receipt and just said, "Page 214 is where it gets good." So I checked page 214, obviously, but honestly nothing especially interesting happened there.

**Interviewer:**  
What did you do with it? Did you leave anything back?

**Participant:**  
I left the receipt in the book. I thought about writing, "No, it doesn't," underneath it, but that seemed a bit rude, so I didn't.

**Interviewer:**  
Did you wonder who left it?

**Participant:**  
Yeah, mostly because I wanted to know why they liked that page so much. Maybe I missed something. Or maybe the receipt was originally inside a different book and someone moved it. I don't know.

## Question 4 — Noticing what someone nearby was listening to

**Interviewer:**  
Think of the last time you noticed what someone near you was listening to—a friend, a flatmate, a stranger on the bus, a gym speaker. What happened next?

**Participant:**  
Yesterday, actually. A guy near me in the computer lab had music leaking from his headphones. I could hear the drums, but I couldn't tell what the song was. It was a bit annoying because I was trying to finish an assignment. Nothing happened, really. I just moved to another desk.

**Interviewer:**  
Was there something you wanted to say or do but didn't? Why not?

**Participant:**  
I wanted to ask him to turn it down, but moving seemed easier. He might not even have realised anyone else could hear it, and I didn't want to start a whole conversation about it.

## Question 5 — Passing a song on to someone

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the last time you passed a song on to someone—or wanted to and didn't. Who was it, and what happened afterwards?

**Participant:**  
I sent a song to someone from my group project. We were working late and she said she needed new study music, so I sent her this lo-fi track I sometimes use. I can't remember the name. It had a little animated frog on the cover. She said, "Thanks, I'll listen later," and that was it. I don't know if she actually listened to it. She never mentioned it again.

**Interviewer:**  
How did you feel about what the song might say about you?

**Participant:**  
I didn't really care because it was just study music. If it had been one of my favourite songs, maybe I would've cared more. If someone hates a song you really like, it feels slightly personal, even though it probably isn't.

## Closing

**Interviewer:**  
Is there anything about music and places we haven't talked about that you think I should know?

**Participant:**  
Um... no, I don't think so. Sorry, I'm trying to think of something. Some shops play music way too loudly, I guess. There's a bubble tea place near campus where it's almost impossible to order because you can't hear the staff. But, yeah, that's probably it.

**Interviewer:**  
That's all right. Thanks so much.

**Participant:**  
No worries.
