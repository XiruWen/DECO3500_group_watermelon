# Interview Transcript 2

## Question 1

**Interviewer:**\
Tell me about a recent time a place made you think of a song or want to
listen to certain music. What happened?

**Participant:**\
I remember going to a park after a pretty stressful day at uni. I wasn't
really planning to listen to anything specific, but once I got there, it
was quiet and there were a lot of people just walking or sitting around.
I felt like I needed something relaxing, so I put on a playlist with
songs I already knew.

I think the place influenced me, but mainly because of how it made me
feel. If I had gone there on a really exciting day, I probably would
have chosen completely different music.

**Interviewer:**\
So is your mood more important than the location itself?

**Participant:**\
I think so. The location sets the context, but my mood decides what kind
of music feels right. The same park could work with very different music
depending on the day.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 2

**Interviewer:**\
Think of the last time you listened to music while travelling to or
arriving somewhere. How did it shape your experience of that place?

**Participant:**\
I usually listen to music on the bus or train, especially when I'm going
somewhere unfamiliar. It makes the journey feel less boring, but it also
changes how I see the place when I arrive.

For example, if I'm listening to something calm while travelling, I tend
to notice the surroundings more. If I'm listening to something
energetic, the whole journey feels faster and more exciting.

Sometimes the song becomes part of my memory of the place. Later,
hearing that song can remind me of the journey, even if I wasn't
specifically trying to create that memory.

**Interviewer:**\
Does the weather affect what you listen to when you're travelling?

**Participant:**\
Yes, quite a lot. I'm definitely affected by weather. On a sunny day, I
usually want something happy or upbeat. When it's raining, I normally
choose something slower. The weather changes my mood, so it indirectly
changes my music choice too.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 3

**Interviewer:**\
Describe a place that holds a strong memory or feeling for you. What
music or sounds do you associate with it, and why?

**Participant:**\
My old university library reminds me of a particular period when I was
studying a lot. I used to listen to the same few albums there, mostly
because they helped me concentrate.

Now when I hear those songs, I immediately remember sitting in the
library and working. It's interesting because the songs themselves don't
really have anything to do with the library. It's just that I listened
to them there so many times.

**Interviewer:**\
Would you say the music reminds you more of the place or the feeling you
had at that time?

**Participant:**\
Probably the feeling. The place is part of the memory, but I mainly
remember how I felt when I was there. The music brings that feeling
back.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 4

**Interviewer:**\
Tell me about a time music you were listening to clashed with your
surroundings or mood. What made it feel wrong, and what would have fit
better?

**Participant:**\
One time I was working on an assignment and had a really energetic
playlist on. At first I thought it would help me stay motivated, but
after a while it became distracting. I couldn't concentrate because I
was paying too much attention to the songs.

I eventually changed to something instrumental and much calmer. That
worked better because it supported what I was doing instead of competing
with it.

**Interviewer:**\
Would you normally choose music based on what you're doing?

**Participant:**\
Yes. The activity matters a lot. If I'm exercising, I want something
energetic. If I'm studying, I want something that can stay in the
background. If I'm just walking around, I have more freedom to listen to
whatever I feel like.

**Interviewer:**\
Do you think your music choices say something about your personality?

**Participant:**\
Maybe a little, but I wouldn't say they define me. My music changes
depending on my mood and situation. Someone could see me listening to
really upbeat music one day and assume I'm an energetic person, but that
might not be true at all. It could just be that I was having a good day.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 5

**Interviewer:**\
What influences your music choices across different places, like home,
uni, transport, parks, or social spaces? Give me a specific example.

**Participant:**\
At home, I normally choose music based on what I'm doing. If I'm cooking
or cleaning, I like something more upbeat because it makes the activity
more enjoyable. When I'm studying, I choose something quieter.

On public transport, I usually choose music based on my mood and the
weather. If it's a really nice day, I might listen to something that
feels more positive.

When I'm with friends, it's different because the music becomes
something we choose together. Someone might play a song I've never heard
before, and that can lead me to discover something new.

**Interviewer:**\
How do you feel when a friend recommends a song that you don't like?

**Participant:**\
I'm usually more willing to give it another chance. If a random
recommendation doesn't work for me, I'll probably skip it immediately.
But if it's from a friend, I might listen again because I'm curious
about why they like it.

Sometimes I actually end up liking the song after hearing it a few more
times.

**Interviewer:**\
Does knowing who selected the song change how you experience it?

**Participant:**\
Yes. When I know the person, I start thinking about the reason they
chose it. Maybe it reminds them of something, or maybe they think it
suits the situation. So I'm not only listening to the song. I'm also
thinking about the person who selected it.

**Interviewer:**\
Would you be interested in discovering music through people around you
in the same place?

**Participant:**\
Yes, especially if there was some context about why they chose it. I
think that would make it more interesting than just getting another
random recommendation. You could discover a song and also learn
something about the person who chose it.
