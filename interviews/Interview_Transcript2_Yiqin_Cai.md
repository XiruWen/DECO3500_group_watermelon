# Interview Transcript

**Participant profile:** Postgraduate music composition student; part-time studio assistant to a music creator; based in Hangzhou.

---

## Question 1

**Interviewer:**
Firstly, could you please tell me about a recent time a place made you think of a song or want to listen to certain music. What happened?

**Participant:**
The lift in our studio building. It's an old building near Xixi, and the lift is so slow that I've actually timed it. Ground floor to the sixth floor is exactly one chorus and half a bridge of this one song. So now that's the lift song. If someone else gets in and presses the third floor, it ruins everything.

I know that sounds silly, but I do it every morning. It's like a little ritual before I go in and become "the assistant" for the day.

**Interviewer:**
So would you say the place itself or your mood is more important?

**Participant:**
The place, because my mood is basically decided by whether my teacher liked yesterday's demo, and I don't find that out until I'm out of the lift. So the song has to come before the mood. The lift doesn't care about the demo.

## Question 2

**Interviewer:**
Think of the last time you listened to music while travelling to or arriving somewhere. How did it shape your experience of that place?

**Participant:**
Honestly, most of the time I'm not listening to music for fun when I travel. I'm checking mixes. My teacher sends me unfinished tracks and says "listen to this on the way, tell me what you think." So I ride a shared bike along the Su Causeway with a demo in my ears, and I'm thinking "the bass is too much" instead of looking at the lake.

There's a thing people do called the car test, listening to a mix in the car to see if it holds up. We have the West Lake test. Once I realised the reverb on a vocal felt way too big because the lake was already so open and quiet. We cut it by half the next day.

**Interviewer:**
Does that make you more likely to listen to the same song again when you visit that place?

**Participant:**
The opposite, kind of. Now some parts of the lake are "work" to me. There's one bench near Xiling Bridge where I sat and listened to the same eight bars maybe forty times. I can't sit there anymore without hearing those eight bars.

So this year I made a rule: weekends, no demos near the lake. I just listen to whatever is actually there. Mostly it's aunties doing group dancing and someone's Bluetooth speaker. It's honestly better than some of our demos.

## Question 3

**Interviewer:**
Describe a place that holds a strong memory or feeling for you. What music or sounds do you associate with it, and why?

**Participant:**
My grandma's flat in Chongqing. The light rail goes very close to the building, and every few minutes the whole room hums a little. As a kid I thought the building was breathing. Plus mahjong tiles from the neighbours every evening, that washing sound when they shuffle.

No song. Just the train and the tiles.

**Interviewer:**
Is there anywhere here that's like that yet?

**Participant:**
Funny, actually, yes. The air conditioner in our studio has this low hum, and one time we forgot to cut it out of a recording. My teacher listened back and said, keep it, it sounds like a room. So it's on a released track now.

So technically my workplace is on a record. I'm not credited, the air conditioner isn't credited either. But every time I hear that track, I know exactly which corner it came from.

## Question 4

**Interviewer:**
Tell me about a time music you were listening to clashed with your surroundings or mood. What made it feel wrong, and what would have fit better?

**Participant:**
We went to the tea fields at Meijiawu to do some field recording, and I had something very slow and ambient on while we were walking up. Very "I'm an artist in nature." And then a tour group came round the corner with a guide shouting through a little loudspeaker and a song from a TV drama playing on someone's phone.

I just took my earphones out and laughed. That was the real soundtrack of the place. My ambient thing was a version of the tea fields that only existed in my head.

**Interviewer:**
Did it feel embarrassing, or just funny?

**Participant:**
Mostly funny. A little bit embarrassing, because I realised I was trying to make the place into an album cover. The tour group was just enjoying the day. They were more "in" the place than me.

**Interviewer:**
Would the weather or environment make a difference?

**Participant:**
In Hangzhou, yes, a lot. During plum rain season everything is wet for weeks, and I want warm, close music, like being under a blanket. And at night, if I walk back through the wetland paths, I keep one earphone out. Partly safety, partly mosquitoes. You want to hear them coming.

## Question 5

**Interviewer:**
Finally, what influences your music choices across different places, like home, uni, transport, parks, social spaces? Please give me a specific example.

**Participant:**
Whether I'm using my work ears or my own ears. At the studio and on the way there, it's work ears, everything is a reference track. At home it's my own ears, and I'll listen to really cheesy stuff I'd never admit to my classmates.

On the metro I'm in the middle. Line 1 is so crowded that nobody can see my screen anyway, so I'm quite free there.

**Interviewer:**
Do your friends influence what you listen to?

**Participant:**
My teacher, definitely, but that's a different kind of influence. If he sends me something, I have to finish it, because tomorrow he'll ask "what did you think of the drums at 2:14?" So I listen very carefully, but it's a bit like homework.

With classmates it's more relaxed. Half the time they're sending their own songs and I have to say something nice.

**Interviewer:**
What about something from a stranger, like someone you don't know at all?

**Participant:**
I'd love that, honestly. With a stranger there's no pressure to have an opinion. I'd just listen.

But I'd want a tiny bit of context. Like, were they walking or stuck in traffic on Wen Yi Road? Was it raining? Otherwise it's just the recommendation page again, and I already ignore that.

**Interviewer:**
Would you enjoy a system where you could discover music through what people around you are listening to?

**Participant:**
Yes, it would be like finding someone's note inside a library book.

But I wouldn't leave anything at the studio. My teacher would find it in about a day and ask why I'm listening to that. Also, anyone could figure out I'm there every weekday from nine. Maybe I'd leave something at Broken Bridge, because a million people go there and nobody would know it was me.

**Interviewer:**
So you'd use it differently in different places?

**Participant:**
I think I'd take things from small places and leave things in big touristy places. Which is maybe the wrong way round for everyone else, haha.

And if I opened it at Broken Bridge and there was nothing, I'd think the whole app was dead. Because Broken Bridge is never empty.
