## Opening

**Interviewer:**  
Thanks for doing this. I'm doing a uni project about how people come across music in everyday life. There are no right answers—I'm interested in your stories. It'll take about 15 minutes. Is it OK if I record this? It's only for our team, and your name won't appear anywhere.

**Participant:**  
Yeah, absolutely. That's totally fine. I talk a lot, though, so you might have to stop me.

## Warm-up

**Interviewer:**  
That's all right. To start, where do you usually listen to music during a normal week?

**Participant:**  
Pretty much everywhere, honestly. I listen while I'm getting ready in the morning, driving, cooking, working out, whatever. At work, my team has a shared speaker, so we take turns choosing music. I'm usually the annoying person asking, "Wait, who is this?" and adding songs to my phone. I also go to gigs when I can, and my friends have people over quite often, so there's normally music playing somewhere.

## Question 1 — A place where a song is stuck for you

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a place—a room, a shop, a walk, a bus route—where a specific song or artist is stuck for you. What happened there?

**Participant:**  
Oh, yeah, New Farm Park and Maggie Rogers. A few of us had a picnic there for my friend's birthday, and someone played *Light On*. It had been raining all week, but that afternoon was suddenly really sunny. We had cheap wine, one of those supermarket cakes, and a speaker that kept cutting out. When that song came on, my friend grabbed me and started dancing. It was very silly. There were families nearby probably wondering what we were doing.

**Interviewer:**  
Was it the place itself, or how you were feeling at the time, that made it stick?

**Participant:**  
Um, probably how I was feeling. I'd had a pretty bad week, actually, and I almost didn't go. Then I got there and everyone was being ridiculous, and I felt much better. But the park is part of it too. Like, if I hear that song somewhere else, I still picture that afternoon.

**Interviewer:**  
What happens now when you go back there?

**Participant:**  
I normally send a photo to that friend and say, "Our song is playing in my head again." She usually replies with a dancing GIF. We've done that a few times.

## Question 2 — Music chosen by a person

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a time you were somewhere and could tell the music had been chosen by an actual person rather than an algorithm. How did you know?

**Participant:**  
At my friend's barbecue, definitely. She had a playlist, but then everyone kept adding songs to the queue. You could tell exactly who had chosen what. Her brother kept adding nineties hip-hop, someone else added Taylor Swift, and my friend's dad put on Fleetwood Mac twice because he forgot he'd already played it. It was chaotic, but in a good way.

**Interviewer:**  
What did you end up thinking about whoever chose it?

**Participant:**  
I mean, I already knew most of them, but some choices were surprising. One of the quietest people there put on this really dramatic dance track and knew every word. I remember thinking, "Okay, there's clearly another side to you." Then I asked her for more songs, and she sent me a whole playlist the next day.

**Interviewer:**  
Did it matter whether you knew that person?

**Participant:**  
Not really. Sometimes it's actually more fun when I don't know them because then I can ask about the song and start talking to them. I do that quite a lot. Maybe more than most people.

## Question 3 — Something a stranger left behind

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the last time you came across something a stranger had left behind for others to find—a note, a book in a street library, a message on a wall, anything.

**Participant:**  
There's a community noticeboard outside a cafe near my place, and someone had put up a little handwritten list called "Songs for walking home at night." It wasn't advertising anything. It was literally just five songs and a tiny drawing of a moon. I took a photo because I thought it was cute.

**Interviewer:**  
What did you do with it? Did you leave anything back?

**Participant:**  
I listened to the songs on the walk home. I liked three of them, I think. The next time I went past, I added another song at the bottom. I had a pen in my bag, which is unusual because I can normally never find one when I need it.

**Interviewer:**  
Did you wonder who left it?

**Participant:**  
Yeah, definitely. I wondered if they lived nearby and whether they'd come back to check if anyone had added anything. I went back a few days later, actually, but the whole notice had disappeared. Maybe the cafe removed it. That was a bit disappointing.

## Question 4 — Noticing what someone nearby was listening to

**Interviewer:**  
Think of the last time you noticed what someone near you was listening to—a friend, a flatmate, a stranger on the bus, a gym speaker. What happened next?

**Participant:**  
I was waiting for a drink at a bar, and the woman next to me was showing her friend a song on her phone. I recognised the artist, so I said, "Sorry, are you listening to Remi Wolf?" She said yes, and then we started comparing favourite songs. Her friend looked slightly bored, to be honest, because we talked for quite a while. We followed each other on Spotify, but I don't think we exchanged names, which is kind of funny.

**Interviewer:**  
Was there something you wanted to say or do but didn't? Why not?

**Participant:**  
No, I usually just say it. If someone seems busy or they've got headphones on, I won't bother them. But if the music is already playing out loud, I don't think it's that strange to ask. I've probably embarrassed myself a few times, but it's fine.

## Question 5 — Passing a song on to someone

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the last time you passed a song on to someone—or wanted to and didn't. Who was it, and what happened afterwards?

**Participant:**  
This morning, actually. My friend sent me a voice message complaining about someone she's dating, and it reminded me of a song, so I sent it to her. It was *Casual* by Chappell Roan. Maybe slightly dramatic for eight in the morning, but it matched the situation. She replied, "This is rude but accurate," and then sent it to the person she was complaining about, which I did not tell her to do.

**Interviewer:**  
Oh, wow. What happened after that?

**Participant:**  
I don't know yet. She hasn't replied for about two hours, so either they're having an important conversation or she's just at work. Hopefully the second one.

**Interviewer:**  
How did you feel about what the song might say about you?

**Participant:**  
I don't worry about that much. I share songs all the time, so I don't think every song represents me. Sometimes it just reminds me of a person or something they said. Although, yeah, if I send someone a really romantic song, I'll probably add a message explaining why, just so they don't misunderstand me.

## Closing

**Interviewer:**  
Is there anything about music and places we haven't talked about that you think I should know?

**Participant:**  
Um, let me think. I use Shazam constantly when I'm out. Sometimes I'll be in the middle of a conversation and suddenly hold up my phone because I want to catch the song before it ends. My friends hate it because I stop listening to them for about ten seconds. Also, Shazam never works when the song is actually good. It only works when it's something I don't care about. I don't know if that helps, but that's the first thing I thought of.

**Interviewer:**  
That's great. Thanks so much.

**Participant:**  
No worries. That was fun.
