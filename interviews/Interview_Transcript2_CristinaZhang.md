## Notes by question

### Q1 — A place where a song is stuck for you
- Most often happens **on the way to uni**.
- After hearing a song they like while travelling, the melody keeps playing in their head while they do other things.
- They put this down to **liking the song**, and said it has **nothing to do with any particular place**.

### Q2 — Music you could tell a person had chosen
- In some **restaurants and shops**, they can tell the music was picked on purpose to match the atmosphere of the place.
- Attitude: they **approve of it**.
- But they have **no wish to connect with whoever chose it**: that person is staff, and connecting with them over the music would feel odd.

### Q3 — Something a stranger left behind for others
- **Graffiti on walls** in the street.
- They did not want to respond or make any connection, **because they cannot do graffiti themselves**.
- They added that it **depends on the situation**: if responding were easy, they might want to. What stops them now is mainly **the form** (graffiti) and **how convenient it is** to respond.

### Q4 — Noticing what someone near you was listening to
- Their **flatmate was playing audio out loud**, and they heard it.
- No further detail recorded.

### Q5 — Passing a song on to someone
- They shared **a song they found interesting with their flatmate**.
- No further detail recorded.

## Research insights

| Evidence (paraphrased) | Initial code | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| A song stays in their head because they like it, not because of where they heard it | Song preference outweighs place | **Place alone does not carry meaning** |
| Can tell shop and restaurant music was chosen to fit the atmosphere, and approves | Notices human curation | **Human curation is noticed and valued** |
| No wish to connect with the staff who chose it; it would feel odd | Valuing a choice ≠ wanting to meet the chooser | **Recognition without connection** |
| Did not respond to graffiti because they cannot do graffiti | Response blocked by the medium | **Barrier to responding** |
| Would consider responding if it were easy | Effort determines response | **Barrier to responding** |
| Heard flatmate's music through a shared space; shared a song with the same flatmate | Awareness and sharing inside a close tie | **Sharing follows existing relationships** |


## Implications to test

1. **An echo needs context, not just a location.** Ask participants whether a short "why I left this here" changes whether they would listen. This matches Voida et al. (2005) in `literature/`.
2. **Answering must be as easy as finding.** P2 did not answer graffiti because the medium demanded a skill they lack. A reply in EchoSpot should take one step and need no creative effort, for example replying with a song.
3. **Contact with the person who left an echo should be optional.** Appreciating what someone left is not the same as wanting to meet them.

## Limitations

These are brief notes. Q4 and Q5 have no follow-up detail, and no probes were recorded, so this interview should carry less weight than a full transcript.
