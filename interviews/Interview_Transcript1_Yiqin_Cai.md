## Question 1

**Interviewer:**
Firstly, could you please tell me about a recent time a place made you think of a song or want to listen to certain music. What happened?

**Participant:**
For me it's very specific places, not moods. There's a bus stop where I wait maybe four times a week, and there's a song I have only ever listened to at that bus stop. I don't play it anywhere else. It's not a rule I made, it just ended up that way.

I think it started because the first time I waited there I was completely lost. I'd only been here about three weeks. And I was standing there for twenty-five minutes because I'd read the timetable wrong, and that song was what was playing.

Now when I'm there I put it on almost automatically. If I'm running late and I don't have time, it feels slightly off.

**Interviewer:**
So would you say the place itself or your mood is more important?

**Participant:**
The place. Definitely the place. My mood changes every day but that bus stop doesn't.

Actually I think it's because I'm new here. When I was at home, all the places were already full of things. I'd been to them my whole life. Here everything was blank when I arrived, so whatever I put into a place is what's in it. There isn't anything competing.

## Question 2

**Interviewer:**
Think of the last time you listened to music while travelling to or arriving somewhere. How did it shape your experience of that place?

**Participant:**
I use it to make places less frightening. That sounds dramatic, but when I first arrived I didn't really want to be outside. Everything was loud and I couldn't tell what anything was. Putting headphones in made it manageable.

The problem is I think I've missed things because of it. My flatmate knows the names of shops, the guy who works at the fruit place, she's had conversations. I've walked past the same shops two hundred times with headphones in.

**Interviewer:**
Does that make you more likely to listen to the same song again when you visit that place?

**Participant:**
Yes, but I want to be honest that it's a bit of a loop. I listen to the same thing in the same place, so the place stays the same. It doesn't get any newer.

That's actually something I've been trying to change this year. I've started leaving the headphones out sometimes on the walk home, just to see what happens. Not much happens. But the walk is different.

## Question 3

**Interviewer:**
Describe a place that holds a strong memory or feeling for you. What music or sounds do you associate with it, and why?

**Participant:**
The stairwell in my old building in Nanjing. It had a sensor light that was broken, so it only came on if you stamped. Every single person in that building stamped on the third landing. You'd hear it through the wall, like stamp, light, footsteps.

I don't have a song for that. It's the sound itself.

**Interviewer:**
Is there anywhere here that's like that yet?

**Participant:**
Not really. Not yet.

There's the café where I work. There's a coffee grinder that makes a very particular noise and I'll probably miss it eventually. But it's not the same because it's not mine, it's the shop's.

I think it takes longer than a year. Or it takes something happening there.

## Question 4

**Interviewer:**
Tell me about a time music you were listening to clashed with your surroundings or mood. What made it feel wrong, and what would have fit better?

**Participant:**
At work, all the time, but that's not my choice. The shop plays whatever the owner picks and it's on a four-hour loop. By the third loop you're not hearing it, you're just enduring it.

A more interesting one: I was walking besides Qiantang river in the evening, quite busy, families around, and I had something very loud and heavy playing. And I remember feeling almost embarrassed, which is stupid because nobody could hear it.

**Interviewer:**
Why embarrassed, if nobody could hear it?

**Participant:**
Because I was the only one in that version of the place. Everyone else was in the actual evening and I was somewhere else. It felt like I was doing something slightly antisocial.

Which is odd, because on the bus I want exactly that. On the bus I want to be somewhere else.

**Interviewer:**
Would the weather or environment make a difference?

**Participant:**
Less than time of day, I think. After dark I'm more careful. I turn the volume down or take one earphone out, because I want to hear what's behind me. That's not about the music at all.

## Question 5

**Interviewer:**
Finally, what influences your music choices across different places, like home, uni, transport, parks, social spaces? Please give me a specific example.

**Participant:**
Whether other people can tell. That's the main one, honestly.

At home nobody knows what I'm playing, so I'll listen to anything. In public I'm still on my own, but I'm aware of myself more. I stand differently when I'm listening to something I like. I've caught myself.

**Interviewer:**
Do your friends influence what you listen to?

**Participant:**
Yes, but it depends who. There's one friend whose recommendations I'll always at least finish, because her taste is completely unlike mine and it's usually something I wouldn't have found. Other people send me things and I'll say thank you and not open it.

**Interviewer:**
What about something from a stranger, like someone you don't know at all?

**Participant:**
That's different, and I think I'd be more curious, not less. With a friend I can guess why they chose it. With a stranger I'd have no idea, so I'd actually listen properly to try and work it out.

But I'd want to know something. Not their name, just, were they here at night, were they alone, how old are they roughly. Otherwise it's the same as an algorithm to me. A song with nobody behind it.

**Interviewer:**
Would you enjoy a system where you could discover music through what people around you are listening to?

**Participant:**
I like the idea of finding something someone left. That's nice, it's like the stamp on the stairwell, in other words, evidence that other people are here.

But I'd have to think about whether I'd put anything in. Because if I leave a song at my bus stop, and I'm there four times a week at the same time, then anyone who wants to work out where I am can do it. I'm not sure I'd want that at my bus stop. Maybe somewhere I only go once.

**Interviewer:**
So you'd use it differently in different places?

**Participant:**
I think I'd take from everywhere and leave things only in places that aren't mine. Which is a bit selfish when I say it out loud.

And the other thing, if I went to a place and there was nothing there, I'd probably not open it again. I'd assume it was empty everywhere.
