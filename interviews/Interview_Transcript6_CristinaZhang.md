## Opening

**Interviewer:**  
Thanks for doing this. It's for a university project about how people come across music in
everyday life. It's only used by our team of five, your name won't appear anywhere, and I
won't write down your employer or any unreleased product. If anything is commercially
confidential, just say skip. Is it all right if I record?

**Participant:**  
Yes, that's fine.

## Question 1 — A feature whose content had to come from users

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a feature you worked on where the content had to come from the users
themselves. Take me through the first week after it went live — what was actually in there,
and what did the team do about it?

**Participant:**  
On our side the product has always put a lot of weight on users sharing things and on
content spreading by itself. At the time we were giving users an incentive to write
comments on songs — to leave their own comment on the line of lyrics that had got to them —
and then share that out.

## Question 2 — A decision about where or when something reaches a listener

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a time a product decision was made about where or when something reaches a
listener — tied to a place, a moment, a scene, a time of day. What was the thinking, and
what happened to it in the end?

**Participant:**  
We have a Daily Fortune feature. It only shows the first time a user opens the app that
day, and there's a push notification or a pop-up in the morning slot. The idea is that you
open it while you're commuting, or when you're going to look at your daily recommendations.
So it doesn't disturb people too much.

## Question 3 — A social feature used in a way the team had not planned for

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a social feature in a music product — yours or someone else's — that you
watched people use in a way the team had not planned for. What did they do with it?

**Participant:**  
We had a personality test a while back. It played you some music clips and then told you
you're such-and-such a personality type. But because people really liked the styles and the
characters we'd made, later on some of them even made fan works about those personalities —
fan art, and people writing their own storylines for them, that sort of thing.

## Question 4 — The first time user content became a problem

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the first time something users had posted became a problem. What was it, who
noticed, and what did you end up changing?

**Participant:**  
There was malicious content, but the safety system caught it, so it didn't really spread.

## Question 5 — An idea that changed shape because of rights

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about an idea that had to change shape because of rights or licensing. What was the
original, what did it become?

**Participant:**  
Our campaigns have always been built on a licensed basis. Of course, if a licence we held
before expires, then whatever those campaigns produced goes dark along with it — I mean the
link that took you to the song. But there can be substitutes — our platform also has covers
and things like that.

## Question 6 — When the numbers and your own judgement disagree

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a time the numbers said a feature was working and your own sense of it
disagreed — or the other way round. What did you do?

**Participant:**  
Sometimes a campaign gets talked about very widely but the interaction numbers are very
low. At that point we pretty much know this one hasn't worked. Because what we care about
all along is users sharing it themselves. If interaction isn't high enough but views are,
that probably means a KOL at the top has blown up. But fundamentally what we want is for
users to spread it themselves — for example, sharing it so that a friend takes part in our
campaign or the test. Posts that simply go viral have limited long-term value for us.

## Question 7 — A place where a song is stuck for you

*(Asked of the participant as a person rather than as a planner — the same question put to
P2–P5.)*

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a place where a specific song or artist is stuck for you. What happened there,
and what happens now when you hear it somewhere else?

**Participant:**  
What does "stuck there" mean... do you mean a song I really like? Well — say a song is tied
up very tightly with something that happened at a particular time. Then when I hear it
again I'll probably think back to what was going on then. For instance, I had an argument
with my partner once and I went out at night and just had one song —
*Shui Xing Ji* by Guo Ding — on repeat.
Afterwards, whenever I hear that song I always go back to that time. Not that it makes me
sad, exactly — it becomes a kind of memory.
