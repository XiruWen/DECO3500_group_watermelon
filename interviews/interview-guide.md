# Interview guide

**Purpose:** collect first-hand experience, not answers to questions.
Per the Week 4 lecture: *"Find the best stories, don't compile answers to questions."*

## Question criteria (from the studio brief)

Every question must:
- **a.** not be answerable in a single word or short phrase
- **b.** produce stories — characters, people, episodes
- **c.** be specific enough to evoke a **particular** experience
- **d.** reveal values relevant to our domain

## Core questions

1. **Tell me about the last time you found an artist you now love but who almost nobody
   around you has heard of.** Walk me through how you came across them — where you were,
   what you were doing, and what made you keep listening.

2. **Think about the last time you went looking for new music and gave up, or ended up back
   with something you had already heard.** What were you doing, what did you try, and where
   did it break down?

3. **Tell me about a place — a venue, a shop, a room, a particular walk — where a specific
   artist or song is stuck for you.** What happened there, and what happens now when you hear
   it somewhere else?

4. **Tell me about a time you were somewhere and could tell the music had been chosen by an
   actual person rather than an algorithm.** How did you know, and what did you end up thinking
   about whoever chose it?

5. **Tell me about the last time you passed something you loved on to someone — or wanted to
   and didn't.** Who were they, what did you choose, and what happened afterwards?

### What each question tests

| Q | Tests | Reveals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discovery channels | Whether discovery runs through people or algorithms |
| 2 | **The channel claim — asks about failure, not success** | Tests our premise without leading the interviewee |
| 3 | **Place ↔ music binding** | Whether the core assumption of EchoSpot holds at all |
| 4 | Human curation vs algorithm | The core value, asked without interrogating a product |
| 5 | Sharing | Vulnerability; where the impulse to connect stops short |

## Probes — bringing the conversation to social & mobile issues

- Where were you when that happened — were you together, or in different places at different times?
- Who else was there? Did they know what you were listening to?
- Afterwards, did anything happen between the two of you? Did you ever talk about it again?
- **Was there something you wanted to say in that moment but didn't? Why not?**
- How did you keep track of what they were listening to after that?

> **Do not interrogate a technology.** We are not trying to build a better Spotify or Bandcamp.
> We are trying to understand the issues people have doing what they want to do, and match those
> to the possibilities of social and mobile computing.

## Questions we do not ask

- ❌ "Do you use Spotify or Bandcamp?" — one-word answer, and it interrogates a technology
- ❌ "What features would you want in a music discovery app?" — asks the participant to do our design work
- ❌ "How important is music to you?" — abstract, no particular episode

## Variant set — independent artists

1. **Take me back to the last time someone with no connection to you discovered your music.**
   How did you find out it had happened, and what did it mean to you?
2. **Tell me about a time you played somewhere and it either connected with the room or completely
   didn't.** What happened, and what did you do afterwards?
3. **Tell me about a decision you made about where to put your music** — a platform, a gig,
   something physical. What were you hoping for, and what actually happened?

## Analysis method (Week 4 lecture)

For each story collected, ask:
1. What has the person identified as **story-able** here?
2. Why was this worth telling?
3. What does this story say about the experiences, values or interests of this person —
   and by implication, our domain?

Then identify **problematisations**: how the person frames something as a problem.
*Every problematisation suggests a direction or shape that a solution can take.*
