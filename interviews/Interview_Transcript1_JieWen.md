# Question 1

Interviewer: Firstly, could you please tell me about a recent time a place made you think of a song or want to listen to certain music. What happened?

Participant: Last week, my friend and I went to a café in South Brisbane for coffee. It was quite busy with a cozy, laid-back vibe. The café was playing soft indie pop, and I suddenly felt that this melody paired really well with the sunlight and coffee aroma. So I used Shazam to identify the track and added it right into my Spotify playlist.

Interviewer: So would you say you actively look for these songs, or is it mostly accidental?

Participant: I wouldn't spend a lot of time searching from scratch. But if I happen to hear a good song in a café, on RED, or through a friend's recommendation, I'm quite willing to give it a try.

# Question 2

Interviewer: Think of the last time you listened to music while travelling to or arriving somewhere. How did it shape your experience of that place?

Participant: A few days ago, I took the bus to uni with my headphones on, shuffling my Spotify daily mix. Just as the bus was approaching the UQ campus, an upbeat track started playing. At that moment, the usually boring commute felt a bit like a scene from a youth movie, making me dread my early morning class a little less.

Interviewer: If a sad song had been playing instead, do you think your feeling toward the campus would have been different?

Participant: Definitely. If it were a slow song, I probably would have sunk into the anxiety of rushing assignments all the way, feeling unenthusiastic about everything. Music really acts as a mood filter.

# Question 3

Interviewer: Describe a place that holds a strong memory or feeling for you. What music or sounds do you associate with it, and why?

Participant: I think of the UQ library during final exam week. That kind of place is always so quiet that you can only hear typing and page-turning. But when I study, I usually wear headphones playing white noise or instrumentals like lo-fi music, because it helps me block out nearby whispering and get into the zone quickly.

Interviewer: Does hearing lo-fi music now instantly bring back the pressure of studying in your mind?

Participant: Yes! Every time I hear that beat, images of academic papers, laptops, and assignment panic automatically pop into my head. It's totally a conditioned reflex.

# Question 4

Interviewer: Tell me about a time music you were listening to clashed with your surroundings or mood. What made it feel wrong, and what would have fit better?

Participant: Once, my friends and I went out for hot pot. The restaurant was bustling and super lively. But I still had a very melancholic, slow-paced love song playing in my headphones. That vibrant, noisy atmosphere completely clashed with the sadness in my ears, and I felt completely detached from the surroundings. Upbeat pop music or the restaurant's actual ambient sound would have fit so much better.

Interviewer: When you encounter this situation, do you usually take off your headphones immediately, or switch playlists?

Participant: I'll quickly take off one side of the headphones or just turn the music off altogether. Because staying immersed in a sad song while chatting with friends makes it hard to focus on the present moment.

# Question 5

Interviewer: What influences your music choices across different places, like home, uni, transport, parks, or social spaces? Give me a specific example.

Participant: The functionality of the space influences my music choices the most. For example, when relaxing or cleaning my room at home, I might just click on a cozy chill playlist on Spotify; but on my way to uni, to wake myself up, I lean towards listening to pop songs with a stronger beat.

Interviewer: Do you take the initiative to control the music in social settings?

Participant: I usually wouldn't take control because I'm not that deeply into music research. But if the music in the restaurant or car sounds great, I might casually mention to my friend, "Hey, this song has a nice vibe."
