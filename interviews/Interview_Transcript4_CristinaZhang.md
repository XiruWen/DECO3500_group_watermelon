## Opening

**Interviewer:**  
Thanks for doing this. I'm doing a uni project about how people come across music in everyday life. There are no right answers—I'm interested in your stories. It'll take about 15 minutes. Is it OK if I record this? It's only for our team, and your name won't appear anywhere.

**Participant:**  
Yeah, sure. Recording is fine.

## Warm-up

**Interviewer:**  
Great. To start, where do you usually listen to music during a normal week?

**Participant:**  
Um, mostly on the train to work. I also put music on when I'm answering emails, although if I actually need to concentrate, I have to turn it off. Then on Friday or Saturday nights, I'll hear whatever's playing at a bar, a party or someone's place. I don't always choose it. Actually, most of the time I don't.

## Question 1 — A place where a song is stuck for you

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a place—a room, a shop, a walk, a bus route—where a specific song or artist is stuck for you. What happened there?

**Participant:**  
Okay, this is a bit random, but there's a bakery near my old office that always makes me think of Dua Lipa. I used to go there for coffee, and for some reason they played her constantly. Like, every morning. I don't even listen to her that much normally.

**Interviewer:**  
Was it the place itself, or how you were feeling at the time, that made it stick?

**Participant:**  
I think it was probably the routine. Same shop, same coffee, same songs, usually already stressed about work. It all got mixed together. I don't think I was having any deep emotional experience. I just wanted coffee.

**Interviewer:**  
What happens now when you go back there?

**Participant:**  
I don't work near there anymore, so I hardly ever go back. I walked past it a few months ago and mostly just thought, "God, I hated that job." So, yeah, not exactly a happy memory.

## Question 2 — Music chosen by a person

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about a time you were somewhere and could tell the music had been chosen by an actual person rather than an algorithm. How did you know?

**Participant:**  
Probably at a house party. The music was all over the place. It went from Beyonce to some old Australian rock song and then suddenly to the *Shrek* soundtrack. That had to be a person. No normal algorithm would do that.

**Interviewer:**  
What did you end up thinking about whoever chose it?

**Participant:**  
Honestly, I thought they were probably drunk. But everyone loved it. People actually shouted when the *Shrek* song came on, so I assumed there was some joke I didn't understand.

**Interviewer:**  
Did it matter whether you knew that person?

**Participant:**  
Yeah, I think it would've been funnier if I knew them because then I might've understood the choices. I didn't know them, so I was mostly just confused.

## Question 3 — Something a stranger left behind

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the last time you came across something a stranger had left behind for others to find—a note, a book in a street library, a message on a wall, anything.

**Participant:**  
Um, the last thing? There was a note stuck inside the lift in my apartment building. It said, "Please stop leaving rubbish in the hallway," and someone else had written underneath it, "Please stop writing passive-aggressive notes."

**Interviewer:**  
What did you do with it? Did you leave anything back?

**Participant:**  
I took a photo and sent it to my group chat because it was funny. I didn't write anything. I didn't want to become part of whatever argument was happening.

**Interviewer:**  
Did you wonder who left it?

**Participant:**  
Yeah, both messages. I have guesses, but I don't actually know. There are a few people in the building who are always complaining about something.

## Question 4 — Noticing what someone nearby was listening to

**Interviewer:**  
Think of the last time you noticed what someone near you was listening to—a friend, a flatmate, a stranger on the bus, a gym speaker. What happened next?

**Participant:**  
Last weekend, someone at a friend's birthday took over the speaker and started playing really aggressive techno. Before that, people were talking, but then it became so loud that nobody could hear anything. I didn't say anything to them. I just went outside with two friends.

**Interviewer:**  
Was there something you wanted to say or do but didn't? Why not?

**Participant:**  
I wanted to ask them to turn it down, but it wasn't my party and I didn't know them. Also, they seemed very pleased with themselves, so I didn't think the conversation would go well.

## Question 5 — Passing a song on to someone

**Interviewer:**  
Tell me about the last time you passed a song on to someone—or wanted to and didn't. Who was it, and what happened afterwards?

**Participant:**  
I sent a Jungle song to someone I'd met at a party. We'd talked about seeing them live, so the next morning I sent it and said, "This is the one I was trying to remember." They reacted with a thumbs-up. That was it. We never really spoke again.

**Interviewer:**  
How did you feel about what the song might say about you?

**Participant:**  
I did wonder if sending it made me look too interested. That's why I wrote such a boring message with it. I wanted it to sound casual. Maybe it sounded too casual, actually. I don't know.

## Closing

**Interviewer:**  
Is there anything about music and places we haven't talked about that you think I should know?

**Participant:**  
Um, not really. Sometimes I remember the bad music at a place more than the good music. There's one bar my friends like, but they always play everything ridiculously loud, so I try to avoid going there. That's probably all.

**Interviewer:**  
Okay, thanks so much.

**Participant:**  
Yeah, no problem.
