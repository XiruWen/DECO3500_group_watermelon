# Interview Transcript

## Question 1

**Interviewer:**\
Tell me about a recent time a place made you think of a song or want to
listen to certain music. What happened?

**Participant:**\
I think it happens quite often, actually. Usually it's more about the
mood of the place. For example, if I'm walking somewhere and it's really
quiet and peaceful, I tend to want something slower, maybe something
I've listened to before.

I remember recently I was walking around campus after class. It was late
afternoon, and the weather was really nice and sunny. I was in a pretty
good mood, so I wanted to listen to something more upbeat. I think the
weather definitely influenced my choice as well. I'm quite a weather
person. If it's sunny, I usually want happy music, and if it's raining
or gloomy, I tend to choose something more calm.

**Interviewer:**\
So would you say the place itself or your mood is more important when
choosing the music?

**Participant:**\
Probably my mood. The place can influence it, but I think my mood is the
stronger factor. The same place can make me want completely different
music depending on how I'm feeling that day.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 2

**Interviewer:**\
Think of the last time you listened to music while travelling to or
arriving somewhere. How did it shape your experience of that place?

**Participant:**\
I listen to music quite a lot when I'm travelling. I think it changes
how I experience the place because the music almost becomes part of the
journey.

For example, when I'm travelling somewhere new, I might listen to music
that matches how I feel about the trip. If I'm excited, I'll probably
listen to something more energetic. If I'm tired, I'll choose something
quieter.

Sometimes afterwards, when I hear the same song again, I remember that
place or that journey. So I think music can become connected to a
specific place without me intentionally doing it.

**Interviewer:**\
Does that make you more likely to listen to the same song again when you
visit that place?

**Participant:**\
Definitely. If I have a strong memory associated with a song, I might
listen to it again when I'm there. It makes the experience feel
familiar, even if the place itself is new.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 3

**Interviewer:**\
Describe a place that holds a strong memory or feeling for you. What
music or sounds do you associate with it, and why?

**Participant:**\
There are a few places like that for me. Usually it's not really one
specific sound from the place. It's a song I happened to listen to a lot
during that period of my life.

For example, there was a place I used to spend a lot of time at, and I
listened to a particular album quite often when I was there. Now
whenever I hear songs from that album, I immediately think about that
place and how I felt at that time.

I think that's why mood is important. It's not just that the music
describes the place. It reminds me of the emotional state I was in when
I listened to it.

**Interviewer:**\
So the connection is more between the music and your memory of the
place?

**Participant:**\
Yes, exactly. The music kind of carries the memory with it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 4

**Interviewer:**\
Tell me about a time music you were listening to clashed with your
surroundings or mood. What made it feel wrong, and what would have fit
better?

**Participant:**\
I definitely have that experience. Sometimes I'll just have something
playing randomly, but it doesn't match how I feel at all.

For example, if I'm having a stressful day and I'm trying to focus,
having really energetic or aggressive music can actually make it worse.
It feels like the music is demanding too much attention from me.

I would probably switch to something slower or more familiar. I don't
necessarily need the music to completely match my mood, but it shouldn't
work against it.

**Interviewer:**\
Would the weather or environment make a difference in that situation?

**Participant:**\
Yes. Definitely. If it's raining outside, I probably wouldn't choose the
same music as I would on a really sunny day. I'm quite influenced by
weather. Sunny weather makes me want happier music, while rainy weather
makes me more likely to choose something calm.

**Interviewer:**\
So would you say your music choice reflects your personality?

**Participant:**\
Not necessarily. I have quite a broad taste in music. I might listen to
something really energetic one day and something completely different
the next day. I think it says more about my mood or what I'm doing at
that moment than my personality.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 5

**Interviewer:**\
What influences your music choices across different places, like home,
uni, transport, parks, or social spaces? Give me a specific example.

**Participant:**\
The activity I'm doing is probably one of the biggest things. At home,
if I'm studying, I might choose something that I can have in the
background. When I'm travelling, I pay more attention to the music
because it becomes part of the journey.

At uni, it depends on whether I'm working or just walking between
classes. If I'm working, I don't want something that's going to distract
me too much.

Social situations are different again. If I'm with friends, music can
become something we share rather than something I choose by myself.

**Interviewer:**\
Do your friends influence what you listen to?

**Participant:**\
Yes, definitely. I've discovered quite a few songs through friends. It's
actually more interesting when I know who chose the song because I start
thinking about why they like it.

If a friend sends me a song that I don't immediately like, I might still
give it another chance because I know that person. With a random
recommendation, I might just skip it.

**Interviewer:**\
Why do you think knowing the person makes such a difference?

**Participant:**\
Because then the music feels connected to them. I might think, "Why did
they choose this song?" or "What does this remind them of?" You learn
something about the person through their music.

But I don't think I would say the song completely represents their
personality. People have really different tastes, and sometimes you
might like the song but not necessarily understand why they chose it.

**Interviewer:**\
Would you enjoy a system where you could discover music through what
people around you are listening to?

**Participant:**\
Yes, I think that could be interesting, especially if it wasn't just
showing me random songs. If I knew that someone I was with had chosen
the song, I'd be more curious about it. It could make listening to music
feel more like a shared activity rather than just something I'm doing
alone.
