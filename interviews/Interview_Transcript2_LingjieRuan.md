# Interview Transcript 2

**Participant:** P8 (de-identified)
**Interviewer:** Lingjie Ruan
**Background:** Hobbyist musician who plays guitar and produces indie and lo-fi demos at home.

## Question 1

**Interviewer:**
Tell me about the last time someone you did not know discovered your music.

**Participant:**
Someone found one of my demos through a friend who reposted it. They messaged me and said they listened to it while studying late at night.

What I remember most was not that they liked the song, but that they told me when they listened to it. I made the track late at night as well, so it felt like they understood the mood.

**Interviewer:**
Was that more important than getting more listeners?

**Participant:**
For me, yes. I am not trying to become a professional musician. Ten people really connecting with something is probably more interesting to me than getting a lot of random plays.

## Question 2

**Interviewer:**
Tell me about a place that influenced your music.

**Participant:**
There is a small park near where I used to live. I sometimes went there with a guitar in the afternoon.

I wrote part of one song there. You could hear birds and traffic at the same time, and I actually recorded some of the background sound on my phone.

Later I used a very quiet part of that recording in the track.

**Interviewer:**
Why keep the background sound?

**Participant:**
Because it reminds me that the song came from an actual moment. If everything is perfectly clean, sometimes it loses that feeling.

## Question 3

**Interviewer:**
Have you ever played music somewhere and felt it did not fit the place?

**Participant:**
Yes. I once played some of my slower music at a small gathering with friends.

People were talking and drinking, so nobody really wanted to listen carefully. I realised the music was probably fine, but the situation was wrong.

**Interviewer:**
What did you learn from that?

**Participant:**
The same song can work really well in one place and feel completely wrong somewhere else. Context matters a lot.

## Question 4

**Interviewer:**
Tell me about how you normally discover music made by smaller artists.

**Participant:**
Mostly through friends, small online communities, or sometimes when I hear something playing in a café or a small venue.

I like discovering music when there is some story around it. If someone just sends me a link with no explanation, I often forget about it.

**Interviewer:**
What kind of context makes you interested?

**Participant:**
Something simple is enough. Where they heard it, why they liked it, or why they think it fits that place.

That makes it feel like another person is behind the recommendation rather than just an algorithm.

## Question 5

**Interviewer:**
How would you feel about leaving one of your own tracks somewhere for strangers to discover?

**Participant:**
I think I would do it if the place meant something to the song.

For example, if I made part of a track in a park, leaving it there for someone else to find would make sense.

But I probably would not leave music everywhere just to promote myself. That would start to feel like advertising.

**Interviewer:**
Would you want people to know who made it?

**Participant:**
Maybe just a nickname. I would like them to know that a real person left it there, but I would not need my full profile attached to everything.
