# Question 1

Interviewer: Firstly, could you please tell me about a recent time a place made you think of a song or want to listen to certain music. What happened?

Participant: Last week while working as a barista at the café, the coffee grinder next to the bar emitted a rhythmic and dull roaring sound. At a moment when there were no customers, this sound suddenly triggered my inspiration, making me think of a guitar arpeggio progression for a new song I've been writing. I desperately wanted to put down my coffee cup right then and hum the melody into my phone.

Interviewer: As a creator, do you often turn ambient noises from everyday life into songwriting material?

Participant: Yes, ambient sounds often have more vitality than deliberate brainstorming. For instance, rainwater splashing on the street or even the friction sound of a passing subway often become inspiration sources for my arrangements.

# Question 2

Interviewer: Think of the last time you listened to music while travelling to or arriving somewhere. How did it shape your experience of that place?

Participant: A few nights ago, I rode my bike to the rehearsal studio with our band's newly recorded, still-mixing demo playing in my headphones. As the night breeze blew and streetlights flashed backward, listening to the music I created echo in my ears made that desolate street feel less like a mere physical space and more like a miniature stage of my own.

Interviewer: Does listening to your own work make you excited or anxious for the rehearsal?

Participant: Both excited and critical. I'll listen while nitpicking, thinking that once I get to the rehearsal room, I definitely need to dial down the guitar distortion a bit.

# Question 3

Interviewer: Describe a place that holds a strong memory or feeling for you. What music or sounds do you associate with it, and why?

Participant: I think of the backstage area of the small live venue where I first performed on stage. That place was cluttered with gear, and the air smelled of beer and sweat. Whenever I think of it, tuning noises from right before going on stage and the faint applause and shouts of the audience automatically echo in my mind. That's the deepest starting memory as an independent musician.

Interviewer: How does this memory influence your current music creation or mindset?

Participant: It reminds me of why I started writing songs in the first place. Although I often feel frustrated now—like when my music gets very few listeners on Spotify and it's hard for people who genuinely like this style to discover me—thinking of that scene makes me feel I can keep going.

# Question 4

Interviewer: Tell me about a time music you were listening to clashed with your surroundings or mood. What made it feel wrong, and what would have fit better?

Participant: Once while working as a barista at the café during the day, the shop was blasting this really loud, commercial EDM track. I was in a terrible state from staying up late writing songs, and internally I just wanted some quiet. That high-frequency, noisy tempo completely went against my low pressure, making me feel very annoyed. If they had played some soothing acoustic guitar or jazz instrumentals back then, it would have been so much more comfortable.

Interviewer: Did you have the urge to suggest changing the playlist to the store manager?

Participant: Haha, I thought about it, but it's work after all. In the end, I just popped in my in-ear headphones and played some of my favorite indie folk to forcefully drown out the outside noise.

# Question 5

Interviewer: What influences your music choices across different places, like home, uni, transport, parks, or social spaces? Give me a specific example.

Participant: As a creator, the "acoustic environment" of the space I'm in and my occupational state directly determine what I listen to. For instance, when I'm working as a barista during the day, the environment itself is very noisy, so when I'm walking home alone afterwards, I absolutely avoid complex music and instead lean toward listening to minimalist ambient music to clear my head.

Interviewer: Is your biggest current struggle also related to this limitation in music distribution?

Participant: Yes, that hits the exact pain point. Even though I've uploaded my work to Spotify and SoundCloud, natural exposure is extremely limited, and Instagram posts are easily scrolled past in the feed. My biggest difficulty truly is how to let people who genuinely love this niche indie music discover me.
