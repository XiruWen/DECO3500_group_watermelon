## Opening and consent

**Facilitator:**  
Thanks for helping with this prototype test. This is a university project about how people discover sounds that other people have left in everyday places. There are no right answers. Please say what you are thinking, including anything you dislike or do not understand. I would like to record the conversation, but your name will not appear anywhere. You can stop at any time. Is that okay?

**F1:**  
Yeah, that's completely fine.

## Finding task

**Facilitator:**  
You have just come out of the Brisbane Jazz Club. Imagine this is your own phone. Open the prototype and explore anything that catches your attention. Please talk aloud while you do it.

**F1:**  
Okay. Oh, so these are sounds people left on the map? Are you collecting sounds from around Brisbane? That's actually really cool. I can see a few markers, but there's one basically right beside me with a piano icon.

**Facilitator:**  
What are you looking at first?

**F1:**  
The list at the side—well, at the bottom on the phone. The closest one says "That solo by the river." It's only about twenty metres away. That has to be from the jazz club, surely. The title and the piano icon make sense here, so I'll tap that one.

**Facilitator:**  
Before you play it, what do you expect to hear?

**F1:**  
Probably part of tonight's band, or maybe someone playing the piano inside. It might also be someone practising. I don't know. Because it says "solo," I expect a proper piece of music, not someone speaking.

**Facilitator:**  
What else can you see on the screen?

**F1:**  
There's the title, the piano icon, a nickname—"After Hours"—and the distance. There's also a heart. I can see the search radius says one kilometre. I don't think I need to change that because this one is already right here.

**Facilitator:**  
Please continue.

**F1:**  
I'll play it now. Oh, I know this song. That's *Autumn Leaves*, right? Yeah. I really like this one. The recording is a little rough, but I like that you can hear the clapping at the end. It sounds like somebody actually caught a moment from the room, not just uploaded a clean Spotify track.

**Facilitator:**  
What did you do while it was playing?

**F1:**  
I looked back at the club. I was trying to work out whether it was the band I just heard or a different night. I also looked around a bit, like maybe the person who left it was still nearby, but obviously I wouldn't know who they were.

**Facilitator:**  
How does hearing it here affect your reaction?

**F1:**  
It makes it more interesting. If somebody sent me a twelve-second phone recording of *Autumn Leaves* at home, I'd probably say, "Okay, cool," and move on. Here I've just been inside the club, and the river is right there, so the title works. It feels like finding a little leftover piece of another performance.

**Facilitator:**  
Is there anything you want to do after listening?

**F1:**  
I'll tap the heart. I want the person to know somebody liked it. If there were a reply option, I might say, "I love this song too," or leave another jazz track. I don't need to contact them properly. That would be too much for a stranger.

**Facilitator:**  
Would you want more information about the sound?

**F1:**  
Yeah, the date and the band's name. Maybe the song name too, although I recognised it. I don't really care about the sharer's real name. The nickname is enough. I care more about what was happening when they recorded it.

**Facilitator:**  
You mentioned the search radius. Would you move it beyond one kilometre to see what else is available?

**F1:**  
Probably not right now. I'm interested because this marker is right beside the place I'm already at. If I made the circle bigger, I'd get sounds from the city or South Bank or wherever, but I'm not planning to walk over there. One kilometre already feels fairly big.

**Facilitator:**  
What would make you ignore a nearby marker?

**F1:**  
If the title was really vague, like "Listen to this," or if the icon had nothing to do with the place. Here I clicked because the jazz connection was obvious. Also, if there were twenty markers on top of each other, I probably wouldn't check all of them.

**Facilitator:**  
If you could change one thing about this experience, what would it be?

**F1:**  
I would show a short description before playing—something like "live jazz recording from Saturday night." I liked this one, but the title alone doesn't tell me whether it is a real song, a voice message or just background noise.

## Facilitator observation notes

- F1 looked back toward the venue while listening and briefly looked around for the sharer.
- F1 tapped the heart button after listening.
