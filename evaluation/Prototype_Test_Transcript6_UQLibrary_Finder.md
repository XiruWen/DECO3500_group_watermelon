## Finding task

**Facilitator:**  
You have just left the library after studying. Imagine this is your own phone. Open the prototype and explore anything that catches your attention. Please talk aloud while you do it.

**F3:**  
Okay. I can see my location and a bunch of little icons. There's one with headphones really close to the library. The title is "You've done enough today." That's... actually very relevant right now.

**Facilitator:**  
What does the title make you think?

**F3:**  
It sounds like somebody left it for students coming out of the library. Maybe it's telling me to stop studying and go home. It's a little cheesy, but in a nice way. I want to know what song they picked.

**Facilitator:**  
What information can you see before opening it?

**F3:**  
The headphones icon, the title, the nickname and the distance—about thirty metres. The radius is one kilometre. I can see other echoes farther away, but I'm not going to touch the slider because this one is already exactly where I am.

**Facilitator:**  
Please open it.

**F3:**  
I'll tap it... and play. Oh, it's *Paris in the Rain*. I love this song. I used to listen to it all the time. This is a very good choice after studying, actually.

**Facilitator:**  
What are you doing while it plays?

**F3:**  
I'm just standing here listening. I was going to start walking, but now I want to hear the rest of the clip. It's quiet outside, and the lights from the library are behind us. The whole thing works together better than I expected.

**Facilitator:**  
Would the same song have the same effect somewhere else?

**F3:**  
Maybe, but the title wouldn't hit the same way. If I saw "You've done enough today" at a shopping centre, I'd wonder what I was supposed to have done. Here I've literally just spent hours in the library, so it feels like the person understood who might find it.

**Facilitator:**  
What do you think about the person who left it?

**F3:**  
Probably another student. Maybe they were also tired and left a song they use when they need to calm down. I don't need their real name, but I like knowing that someone else stood here and thought, "People coming out of that building need a break."

**Facilitator:**  
What would you do after listening?

**F3:**  
I'll tap the heart. I would also save the song if the app let me. I already know it, but if I didn't, I'd want the actual track name and a way to open it later. I might reply with another calm song, but only if replying was easy.

**Facilitator:**  
Would you explore other echoes by increasing the distance beyond one kilometre?

**F3:**  
No, not at the moment. I like this because it is attached to the library I'm standing beside. If I drag the range farther out, then I'm just browsing songs from random parts of Brisbane. I could do that in any music app. One kilometre is already more than I need on campus.

**Facilitator:**  
What if the title had been the same but the audio was something you disliked?

**F3:**  
I would be disappointed, but the title would still feel thoughtful. I think knowing the actual song or at least the genre before I press play would help. In this case I liked it, so it felt like a nice surprise.

**Facilitator:**  
If you could change one thing about the experience, what would it be?

**F3:**  
Show the track name and let me save it. The custom title is good because it explains the place, but it shouldn't replace the real song information. I want both.

## Facilitator observation notes

- F3 selected the nearby 🎧 marker because its title directly matched the experience of leaving the library after studying.
- F3 recognised *Paris in the Rain*, said they loved the song and stayed in place to finish the clip.
- F3 tapped the heart button and wanted a save or track-link action.
- F3 associated the sharer with another tired student but did not want their real identity.
- F3 noticed the 1.0 km radius and explicitly rejected expanding it because the value came from the immediately surrounding place.
