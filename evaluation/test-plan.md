# Evaluation plan — EchoSpot

| | |
|---|---|
| **Updated** | 2026-09-21, after round 1 |
| **Round 1 (done)** | 6 in-situ sessions — 3 sharers, 3 finders — see `evaluation/findings.md` |
| **Round 2 (this plan)** | Paired sessions on two devices, testing the changes round 1 asked for |
| **Run by** | Chuxin Zhang |

## 1. Why round 2 looks different from round 1

Round 1 split the two roles: three people left an echo and walked away, three arrived later and
found one. That split was the right shape — it is what the concept actually does — and it
surfaced six findings. What it could not do is test anything that was not built: playback
distance, replying and multi-device sharing were all performed by the facilitator. The two
halves also never met, so no echo was followed from the person who left it to the person who
answered it.

Round 2 therefore keeps the two-role structure and changes three things: participants are
tested in pairs, each side uses its own device, and the core rule is enforced by the prototype
rather than by the facilitator.

## 2. What must exist before round 2 runs

| # | Change | Needed for | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Playback unlocks on arrival; default radius in tens of metres | EQ1 | Functional prototype |
| 2 | Echo references a track instead of hosting an audio file | EQ4 | Functional prototype |
| 3 | Audio-type label + "why I left this here" note | EQ2 | Functional prototype |
| 4 | One-tap reply with a track; play count visible to the sharer | EQ3 | Functional prototype |

If a change is not ready, it is simulated by the facilitator and **recorded as simulated** in the
session notes, as in round 1.

## 3. Evaluation questions

| # | Question | What round 1 already told us | What round 2 must settle |
|---|---|---|---|
| EQ1 | Does having to be at the place read as reward or friction? | Finders refused to widen the radius and said distant echoes would make it "just another music app" | Whether people will actually walk to a place they have not already arrived at |
| EQ2 | What does a person need before they will press play? | Title alone is not enough; one mismatch ended the whole session | Whether an audio-type label and a "why" note change which echoes get played, and whether they reduce the disappointment |
| EQ3 | What makes responding feel possible? | Both sides want a one-gesture reply and refuse direct contact | Whether a one-tap reply is actually used when it exists, and what the sharer does when a reply arrives |
| EQ4 | Can people share the music they mean to share? | The upload flow blocks streaming tracks; participants re-recorded audio and worried about copyright | Whether a track reference removes the blockage, and what is lost when the audio is not the sharer's own recording |
| EQ5 | When would people not want this? | Raised unprompted: performing in public, being seen idling, others hearing the audio out loud | Where the experience feels exposing or intrusive in a real place with real bystanders |

## 4. Participants

- **6–8 people**, forming 3–4 sharer/finder pairs. The two halves of a pair must not meet during
  the sessions.
- Recruited as proxies: students and commuters who already pass through the test locations.
  Not team members.
- Each participant does **one** role only, as in round 1.

## 5. Locations

Three everyday places with different social conditions, so EQ5 gets varied answers:

| Place | Condition |
|---|---|
| A quiet campus spot (lawn, outside a building) | Few people, easy to record |
| A busy transport stop | Bystanders, noise, a reason to be standing still |
| A venue or café entrance | Somewhere with a shared reason to be there |

## 6. Session protocol — sharer (about 20 minutes)

1. **Consent, spoken and recorded.** University project about sharing music in everyday places;
   audio recorded, no names, photos without faces, stop at any time.
2. **Context first.** What do you usually do at this spot? How do you usually come across new
   music? (Establishes a baseline before the prototype appears.)
3. **Task.** Leave one echo here, thinking aloud. Do not steer the choice of content.
4. **Observe:** how long the choice takes, whether they record or reference a track, whether
   they fill in the "why" note without being asked, whether bystanders change their behaviour.
5. **Questions.**
   - Why this sound, and why here? Would it still make sense somewhere else?
   - Who do you hope finds it, and who would you rather didn't?
   - What do you hope they do after listening?
   - If nobody ever responded, would you leave another one?
   - Name or nickname, and why?
   - Was there anything you thought about leaving and did not?
6. **Record their prediction** of what the finder will do. It will be compared with what the
   finder actually does.

## 7. Session protocol — finder (about 20 minutes)

1. **Consent**, as above.
2. **Context first.** What do you usually do here? How do you usually find new music?
3. **Task, with no explanation of the concept.** "Someone left a sound in this area. Have a
   look." Hand over the device at a point **100–200 m away** from the drop point, so the walk is
   real and EQ1 is actually tested.
4. **Observe on the way:** whether they walk, hesitate, check the screen, give up. Time the walk.
5. **Observe on arrival:** what they say before pressing play, what they do while it plays,
   whether they look for the person who left it.
6. **Questions.**
   - What did you expect before you pressed play? Was that what you got?
   - Was it worth walking here for? Be honest.
   - What did you want to know about it that the screen did not tell you?
   - What would you do next? (If the reply feature is not built, offer the three cards: reply
     with a song / leave a voice note / do nothing, and ask why the other two were rejected.)
   - When would you not want to receive something like this?
   - Would you leave one yourself, and what?

## 8. Closing the loop

Round 1 could not test what happens when a reply comes back, because the two roles never met.
In round 2, after the finder responds, **return to the sharer** — the same day or the next — and
show them what happened: the play count, and the reply if there is one. Record their reaction.

This is the step that tests whether the loop holds: S1 in round 1 said they would stop leaving
echoes if nothing ever came back.

## 10. Analysis

For each finding, record three things:

```
Finding — one sentence
Evidence — what the participant said or did (direct quote where possible)
Change — what this means we should change next
```

Findings that contradict the concept are kept and reported first. Where a round 2 finding
agrees or disagrees with round 1, say so explicitly — two methods pointing the same way is
stronger evidence than either alone.

## 11. Ethics

- Verbal consent recorded at the start of every session; participants may stop at any time.
- No names; nicknames and de-identified labels only.
- Photographs exclude faces and identifiable bystanders.
- Participants are not asked to upload copyrighted audio; where a track is referenced, only the
  link or identifier is stored.
- Recording in a venue is done only with the venue's permission, following the performer-consent
  concern raised in round 1.

## 12. Known limitations to manage

- Small sample, recruited through the team's own networks.
- The gap between leaving and finding is hours, not days, so the long silence a real user would
  experience is still compressed.
- Anything not yet built is simulated by the facilitator and must be labelled as such in the
  notes.
